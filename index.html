Action Checklist

  • Registration Status: Many states purge voter rolls; verify your status 30 days before every election via vote.gov.
  • Know Your ID:Requirements vary by state. If you lack a photo ID, you may be able to use a utility bill or bank statement in some jurisdictions.
  • Provisional Ballots: If your name is missing from the rolls, you have a federal right to cast a provisional ballot..
  • Intimidation: It is illegal for anyone to block polling entrances or question your choices.
  • Language Assistance: You have the right to bring a translator or request ballots in specific languages under the Voting Rights Act.

The "Stay Safe" Protocol

  • Right to Remain Silent: Clearly state: "I am invoking my 5th amendment right."
  • Search Refusal: State clearly: "I do not consent to a search."
  • Right to Record: You can record in public spaces if you don't interfere.
  • The Terry Stop:Police may "pat down" your outer clothing for weapons if they have reasonable suspicion, but cannot search pockets without more cause.

Protester Rights

  • Public Forums: Sidewalks, parks, and plazas have the highest level of 1st Amendment protection.
  • Dispersal Orders: Police must provide a clear exit path and audible warning before making arrests.
  • Spontaneous Protest: "Breaking news" protests on sidewalks generally do not require advance permits.
  • Heckler’s Veto: The government cannot stop your protest just because others might react violently to it.
  • Digital Safety: Disable biometrics (FaceID) and write a lawyer’s number on your arm before protesting.

Digital Sovereignty

  • Passcodes vs. Biometrics: Police can often force a thumbprint, but generally cannot force a numeric passcode.
  • Border Zones: Within 100 miles of any US border, agents have broader authority to search digital devices.
  • Encryption: Use End-to-End Encrypted (E2EE) apps like Signal so no third party can read your messages.
  • Third-Party Doctrine: Info shared with apps (like location) may be accessible to police without a warrant.
  • Metadata: Photos contain EXIF data (GPS/Timestamps) that can track your history unless stripped.
